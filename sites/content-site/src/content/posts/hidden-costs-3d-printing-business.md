Ask a new print seller what a product costs to make and they'll usually tell you the filament price. That's the cost you can *see*. The costs that decide whether your shop actually makes money are the ones that don't show up on the spool label.

Here are the seven that get forgotten most — roughly in order of how much damage they do.

## 1. Your labor

This is the big one. Slicing, starting the print, removing it, cleaning supports, sanding, assembling, photographing, listing, and packing all take *your* time. For small items, labor is often larger than material and machine costs combined. If you're not paying yourself, you don't have a business — you have a job that pays $0/hour. Pick a real rate and count hands-on minutes.

## 2. Machine depreciation

Your printer is a consumable with a slow fuse. Nozzles, belts, bearings, hotends, build plates, and eventually the whole machine wear out. Spread the printer's cost over the hours you expect it to run and charge a few cents per print hour. A $300 printer over 4,000 hours is ~$0.075/hour — small per print, but it's the fund that buys your *next* printer instead of it coming out of profit.

## 3. Failed prints

Every failure still burned filament, electricity, and machine time — and your labor to clean up the mess. If 1 in 12 prints fails, every good unit needs to carry a share of that waste, or your averages lie to you. Track your real failure rate honestly; it's usually higher than you'd like to admit, especially on tall, thin, or bridge-heavy models.

## 4. Marketplace and payment fees

Etsy takes a listing fee, a transaction fee, *and* a payment-processing cut — and it all comes off the **final sale price**, not your cost. That's why adding a flat markup to cost doesn't give you the margin you think. On a $20 item, Etsy-style fees can be $2+ before you've paid for anything. Always price so your margin survives the fees. (Our [pricing guide](/guides/how-to-price-3d-prints/) shows the exact formula.)

## 5. Packaging and shipping supplies

Boxes, mailers, bubble wrap, tape, labels, thank-you cards, filament to print protective inserts. Individually tiny, collectively a real per-order cost — often $0.50–$2.00 before postage. And if you offer "free shipping," the postage itself is a cost you've folded into price whether you meant to or not.

## 6. Design and prototyping time

The hours spent modeling, tweaking, and test-printing a product before the first sale are real costs. You don't add them to every unit, but you *do* need enough margin and volume to earn them back. A design that only ever sells three copies never repaid the evening you spent dialing it in.

## 7. Rejects, returns, and reprints

Customer says it arrived cracked; a print has a blemish you didn't catch; a color came out wrong. Reprints and refunds happen, and they hit your best-selling items most because they sell the most volume. A small allowance here keeps one bad week from erasing a good month.

## The pattern

Notice what these have in common: **none of them appear on the filament receipt, and all of them are real cash.** Sellers who last are the ones who put every one of these into their price from day one — not the ones with the cheapest filament.
