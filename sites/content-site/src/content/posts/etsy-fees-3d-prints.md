You list a printed item at $25, it sells, and the deposit is smaller than you expected. Here's exactly where the difference went — and why the number most sellers have in their head (about 6.5%) is roughly half the real figure.

## Etsy's three standard fees (US, as of August 2026)

| Fee | Rate | Charged on |
|---|---|---|
| Listing fee | $0.20 | Each listing, each renewal, and each unit of a multi-quantity sale |
| Transaction fee | 6.5% | The **total the buyer pays — including shipping** |
| Payment processing | 3% + $0.25 | The total the buyer pays |

Two details do most of the damage. First, the transaction fee applies to shipping, not just the item. Second, there are *two* percentage fees stacked, not one — 6.5% and 3% — plus $0.45 of fixed cost per order once you add the listing fee to the processing fee.

## A real $25 order, line by line

Item $25.00, shipping charged $5.00. The buyer pays **$30.00**.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Buyer pays | $30.00 |
| Listing fee | −$0.20 |
| Transaction fee (6.5% × $30) | −$1.95 |
| Payment processing (3% × $30 + $0.25) | −$1.15 |
| **Etsy total** | **−$3.30** |
| Actual postage you pay | −$5.00 |
| **Left for the item** | **$21.70** |

So a $25 item nets $21.70 before you've paid for a single gram of filament. That's an **effective 13.2% cut of the item price** — double the 6.5% most people quote.

## Then Offsite Ads happens

If the sale came through Etsy's Offsite Ads, add **15% of the order total** (12% if your shop clears $10,000 in a year — at which point enrollment is mandatory and you can't opt out).

On this order that's another $4.50, leaving **$17.20**. Your effective fee rate on a $25 item just went from 13.2% to **31.2%**.

You can't predict which orders will carry it, which is the real problem: your margin has to survive the ad fee on the orders that get one. Sellers who price with no allowance for it discover the hard way that their best month was also their thinnest.

## Now subtract what the print actually cost

Using the figures from our [pricing guide](../how-to-price-3d-prints/) — a 45 g print, 6 hours, 20 minutes hands-on:

| Cost | Amount |
|---|---|
| Filament (45 g @ $0.022/g) | $0.99 |
| Electricity + machine wear (6 h) | $0.57 |
| Labor (20 min @ $18/hr) | $6.00 |
| Failure allowance (8%) | $0.12 |
| Packaging | $0.75 |
| **Production cost** | **$8.43** |

| Scenario | You keep | Minus cost | Profit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal sale | $21.70 | $8.43 | **$13.27** |
| Offsite Ads sale | $17.20 | $8.43 | **$8.77** |

Both are profitable — which is the good news. The bad news is that the ad-fee version pays you about **$26/hour** for your hands-on time, and the moment your labor estimate slips from 20 minutes to 35, that drops under $16/hour.

## Three things this changes about how you price

1. **Charge shipping deliberately.** "Free shipping" doesn't dodge the fee — Etsy charges 6.5% on whatever the buyer pays either way. What free shipping actually does is bury postage inside your item price, where it's easy to forget you're paying it.
2. **Price for the ad fee, not the average.** If an Offsite Ads order would put you underwater, you're not priced for the business you're actually in.
3. **Watch small items.** Fixed costs of $0.45 per order plus packaging land the same on a $6 keychain as on a $60 lamp. Under about $10, fees and handling eat most of what's left.
